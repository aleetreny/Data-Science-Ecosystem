> **Context:** A mathematically rigorous implementation of **Neural Importance Sampling** designed to address the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) simulation bottleneck by optimizing high-dimensional Phase Space integration.

## 1. The Physics Challenge: Simulation Efficiency

Particle physics event generation is dominated by the evaluation of scattering cross-sections, represented as high-dimensional integrals of complex matrix elements: $I = \int_{\Omega} f(x) \, dx$ Current Monte Carlo (MC) integrators suffer from the **Curse of Dimensionality**. In the 40 MHz collision environment of the HL-LHC, uniform sampling is computationally unsustainable. This project implements **Normalizing Flows** to learn the exact topology of the phase space, providing an analytically exact proposal distribution for integration.

## 2. Mathematical Framework

Unlike standard "Black-Box" generative models, this project utilizes **Diffeomorphic Mappings** $T: z \to x$ to ensure mathematical exactness and density tracking.

### 2.1 The Change of Variables Theorem

By utilizing invertible transformations, the exact probability density $q(x)$ of the neural proposal distribution is evaluated via the Jacobian determinant. This determinant quantifies the local volume distortion required to map a simple Gaussian to a complex physics resonance.

### 2.2 Architecture: RealNVP

The model utilizes the **Real Non-Volume Preserving (RealNVP)** architecture. By employing **Affine Coupling Layers**, the Jacobian matrix is restricted to a triangular form, reducing the computational complexity of the determinant calculation to $O(D)$.

-   **Invertibility unit test:** Verified with a reconstruction error of $\epsilon \approx 10^{-7}$.

## 3. Methodology: Overcoming Mode Collapse

A significant challenge in high-dimensional integration is **Mode Collapse**, where narrow resonances are ignored in favor of broad backgrounds.
